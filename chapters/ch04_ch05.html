CHAPTER 4: Directional Dynamics, Orthogonal Dissipation Splitting, and Drift-Diffusion on $\mathbb{S}^2$

4.1. The Orthogonal Dissipation Splitting

Theorem 4.1 (Orthogonal Dissipation Identity):

On the active vorticity domain $\Omega_M(t) = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^3 : |\omega(x,t)| > M\}$, let $\xi(x,t) = \omega(x,t)/|\omega(x,t)| \in \mathbb{S}^2$. Then the full gradient dissipation tensor splits orthogonally:

$$|\nabla \omega|^2 = |\nabla |\omega||^2 + |\omega|^2 |\nabla \xi|^2. \tag{4.1}$$

Proof. Since $\omega_i = |\omega| \xi_i$, the spatial derivative is $\partial_k \omega_i = (\partial_k |\omega|) \xi_i + |\omega| (\partial_k \xi_i)$. Squaring and summing over $i$ and $k$: $$|\nabla \omega|^2 = \sum_{i,k} \left[ (\partial_k |\omega|)^2 \xi_i^2 + 2 |\omega| (\partial_k |\omega|) \xi_i \partial_k \xi_i + |\omega|^2 (\partial_k \xi_i)^2 \right].$$ Since $\xi \in \mathbb{S}^2$, $\sum_i \xi_i^2 = 1$ and $\sum_i \xi_i \partial_k \xi_i = \frac{1}{2} \partial_k (\sum_i \xi_i^2) = 0$. The cross-term vanishes identically, yielding (4.1). $\blacksquare$

4.2. Space-Time Twisting Dissipation Budget

Integrating (4.1) over space and time yields the fundamental geometric budget:

$$\nu \int_0^T \int_{\Omega_M(t)} |\omega|^2 |\nabla \xi|^2\,dx\,dt \le \frac{1}{2} Q_0 + \int_0^T \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} |S(x,t)| |\omega(x,t)|^2\,dx\,dt < \infty. \tag{4.2}$$

Crucial Geometric Obstacle on the Zero Set: On the complementary domain $\Omega_M^c(t) = \{x : |\omega(x,t)| \le M\}$, the weight $|\omega|^2$ vanishes as $|\omega| \to 0$. Consequently, the twisting dissipation $|\omega|^2 |\nabla \xi|^2$ provides zero control over directional oscillations in regions of low vorticity. Any claim that directional alignment persists uniformly on a macroscopic ball without lower bounds on $|\omega|$ is mathematically false and is explicitly retracted in Chapter 10.

CHAPTER 5: Multi-Scale Biot–Savart Decomposition and Geometric Depletion

5.1. Three-Zone Spatial Decomposition via Smooth Partition of Unity

To eliminate spurious boundary terms on sharp cutoff balls, let $\chi \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^3)$ be a standard smooth radial cutoff function such that $0 \le \chi \le 1$, $\chi(y) = 1$ for $|y| \le 1/2$, and $\chi(y) = 0$ for $|y| \ge 1$. For any dynamic radius $z(t) > 0$ and outer radius $R > 0$, we decompose the Biot–Savart kernel using smooth partitions: $$\mathbf{1} = \chi(y/z(t)) + \left[ (1 - \chi(y/z(t))) \chi(y/R) \right] + \left[ 1 - \chi(y/R) \right] =: \chi_{near}(y) + \chi_{mid}(y) + \chi_{far}(y).$$

5.2. Resolution of Near-Field Derivative Loss via Direct Velocity Gradient Convolution

Lemma 5.1 (Near-Field Strain Bound via Smooth Young Convolution):

Let $u_{near} = K_{near} * \omega$, where $K_{near}(y) = \chi(y/z) \frac{1}{4\pi} \frac{y \times \cdot}{|y|^3}$. Since $\operatorname{div} \omega = 0$, the spatial gradient of velocity satisfies directly: $$\nabla u_{near}(x) = K_{near} * \nabla \omega(x). \tag{5.1}$$ Notice: No integration by parts is performed on $B_z$, and consequently no boundary surface integrals on $\partial B_z$ are neglected. Since $|K_{near}(y)| \le \frac{1}{4\pi} |y|^{-2} \mathbf{1}_{|y| \le z}$, the $L^{6/5}(\mathbb{R}^3)$ norm of $K_{near}$ is evaluated directly in spherical coordinates: $$\|K_{near}\|_{L^{6/5}}^{6/5} \le \left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\right)^{6/5} 4\pi \int_0^z (r^{-2})^{6/5} r^2\,dr = (4\pi)^{-1/5} \int_0^z r^{-2/5}\,dr = \frac{5}{3} (4\pi)^{-1/5} z^{3/5}.$$ Taking the $5/6$ power yields: $$\|K_{near}\|_{L^{6/5}(\mathbb{R}^3)} \le C_1 z(t)^{1/2}, \quad C_1 = \left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{5/6} (4\pi)^{-1/6} \approx 0.985. \tag{5.2}$$ By Young's convolution inequality with $1 + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{6} + \frac{1}{2}$: $$\|S_{near}\|_{L^3} \le \|\nabla u_{near}\|_{L^3} \le \|K_{near}\|_{L^{6/5}} \|\nabla \omega\|_{L^2} \le C_1 z(t)^{1/2} \|\nabla \omega\|_{L^2}. \tag{5.3}$$ Coupling with Gagliardo–Nirenberg $\|\omega\|_{L^3}^2 \le C_{GN}^2 \|\omega\|_{L^2} \|\nabla \omega\|_{L^2} = C_{GN}^2 Q(t)^{1/2} \|\nabla \omega\|_{L^2}$: $$\int_{\Omega_M(t)} |S_{near}| |\omega|^2\,dx \le C_1 C_{GN}^2 z(t)^{1/2} Q(t)^{1/2} \|\nabla \omega\|_{L^2}^2. \tag{5.4}$$ Choosing the dynamic cutoff radius: $$z(t) = \frac{c_* \nu^2}{Q(t)}, \quad c_* = (16 C_1^2 C_{GN}^4)^{-1}, \tag{5.5}$$ the near-field stretching is absorbed entirely into viscous dissipation: $$\int_{\Omega_M(t)} |S_{near}| |\omega|^2\,dx \le \frac{\nu}{4} \|\nabla \omega\|_{L^2}^2. \tag{5.6}$$

5.3. Constantin–Fefferman Geometric Depletion in the Intermediate Shell

Theorem 5.1 (Geometric Kernel Depletion):

Under Hypothesis $H_\alpha$ ($|\xi(x+y) - \xi(x)| \le K_\xi |y|^\alpha$), the intermediate strain kernel satisfies $|K_{A,\xi}(y)| \le C_K K_\xi |y|^{\alpha - 3}$. For any $\alpha > 0$, the intermediate shell integral satisfies:

$$\int_z^R r^{\alpha - 3} r^2\,dr = \int_z^R r^{\alpha - 1}\,dr = \frac{R^\alpha - z^\alpha}{\alpha} \le \frac{R^\alpha}{\alpha} < \infty. \tag{5.7}$$
