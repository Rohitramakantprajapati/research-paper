CHAPTER 6: The Subcritical Regularity Theorem ($\alpha > 1/2$)

Theorem 6.1 (Main Subcritical Regularity Theorem):

Let $u_0 \in H^1(\mathbb{R}^3)$ with $\operatorname{div} u_0 = 0$, $E_0 = \|u_0\|_{L^2}^2$, and $Q_0 = \|\omega_0\|_{L^2}^2$. Let $u \in C([0, T^*); H^1(\mathbb{R}^3))$ be the maximal strong solution. Assume Hypothesis $H_\alpha$ holds on $[0, T^*)$ with localized Hölder exponent $\alpha \in (1/2, 1]$:

$$|\xi(x+y, t) - \xi(x, t)| \le K_\xi |y|^\alpha \quad \text{for all } x \in \Omega_M(t), \; |y| \le R. \tag{6.1}$$

Then the enstrophy $Q(t) = \|\omega(t)\|_{L^2}^2$ satisfies the linear differential inequality:

$$\dot{Q}(t) \le \gamma(t) Q(t), \quad \gamma(t) = \frac{C_A^2 K_\xi^2}{\nu} Q(t) + C_E R^{-5/2} E_0^{1/2}. \tag{6.2}$$

Since $\int_0^T Q(s)\,ds \le \frac{E_0}{2\nu}$ by the Leray–Hopf energy inequality, $\gamma \in L^1(0, T)$ with uniform bound:

$$B(T) := \int_0^T \gamma(s)\,ds \le \frac{C_A^2 K_\xi^2 E_0}{2\nu^2} + C_E R^{-5/2} E_0^{1/2} T < \infty. \tag{6.3}$$

Grönwall's inequality yields $Q(t) \le Q_0 \exp(B(T)) < \infty$ for all $t \in [0, T]$. By Beale–Kato–Majda, $T^* = \infty$, proving global smooth existence.

CHAPTER 7: The Critical Case $\alpha = 1/2$, Logarithmic Loss, and Osgood Closure

7.1. Young's Convolution in $L^{6/5}$ and Logarithmic Divergence

At the critical exponent $\alpha = 1/2$, Young's convolution inequality $\|S_A\|_{L^3} \le \|K_{A,\xi}\|_{L^{6/5}} \|\omega\|_{L^2}$ requires computing:

$$\|K_{A,\xi}\|_{L^{6/5}}^{6/5} \le (C_K K_\xi)^{6/5} 4\pi \int_z^R r^{\frac{6}{5}(1/2 - 3) + 2}\,dr = 4\pi (C_K K_\xi)^{6/5} \int_z^R r^{-1}\,dr = 4\pi (C_K K_\xi)^{6/5} \ln\left(\frac{R}{z}\right). \tag{7.1}$$

Taking the $5/6$ power yields $\|S_A\|_{L^3} \le (4\pi)^{5/6} C_K K_\xi \ln^{5/6}(R/z) Q(t)^{1/2}$.

7.2. The Critical Osgood Theorem

Theorem 7.1 (Critical Regularity and Logarithmic Osgood Closure):

Under Hypothesis $H_{1/2}$ with $\alpha = 1/2$, with core cutoff $z(t) = \frac{c_* \nu^2}{Q(t)}$, the logarithmic enstrophy variable $y(t) = \ln\left( \frac{R Q(t)}{c_* \nu^2} \right)$ satisfies:

$$y(t)^{-5/3} \frac{dy}{dt} \le \frac{c_A K_\xi^2}{\nu} Q(t). \tag{7.2}$$

Integrating over time against $\int_0^T Q(s)\,ds \le \frac{E_0}{2\nu}$ yields:

$$y(t)^{-2/3} \ge y_0^{-2/3} - \frac{2}{3} \Theta_{crit}, \quad \Theta_{crit} = \frac{c_A K_\xi^2 E_0}{2\nu^2}. \tag{7.3}$$

Conclusion: If $\Theta_{crit} < \frac{3}{2} y_0^{-2/3}$ (small-data regime), $y(t)$ remains bounded and the solution is globally smooth ($T^* = \infty$). If $\Theta_{crit} \ge \frac{3}{2} y_0^{-2/3}$, the Osgood integral $\int^\infty y^{-5/3} dy < \infty$ converges, blow-up is unprevented, and unconditional regularity remains strictly OPEN.
