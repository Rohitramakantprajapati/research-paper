2.2. Sharp Gagliardo–Nirenberg Interpolation Inequalities

Proposition 2.1 (Sharp Gagliardo–Nirenberg on $\mathbb{R}^3$):

For any divergence-free vector field $u \in H^1(\mathbb{R}^3)$ with $\omega = \operatorname{curl} u \in L^2(\mathbb{R}^3) \cap \dot{H}^1(\mathbb{R}^3)$:

$$\|\omega\|_{L^3(\mathbb{R}^3)} \le C_{GN} \|\omega\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)}^{1/2} \|\nabla \omega\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)}^{1/2}, \tag{2.1}$$

where the sharp constant is given by $C_{GN} = 3^{-1/4} (4\pi)^{-1/3} \approx 0.3274$.

CHAPTER 3: The Vorticity Formulation, Boundary Vanishing, and the Enstrophy Identity

3.1. Evolution Equation for Vorticity

Taking the curl of the momentum equation (1.1) and using the identity $\operatorname{curl}((u \cdot \nabla)u) = (u \cdot \nabla)\omega - (\omega \cdot \nabla)u$ yields the vorticity transport-diffusion equation:

$$\partial_t \omega + (u \cdot \nabla)\omega = (\omega \cdot \nabla) u + \nu \Delta \omega, \quad \operatorname{div} \omega = 0. \tag{3.1}$$

3.2. Vanishing Boundary Flux and the Fundamental Enstrophy Identity

Proposition 3.1 (Rigorous Enstrophy Identity):

Let $u \in C([0, T); H^1(\mathbb{R}^3)) \cap C^1((0, T); H^1(\mathbb{R}^3))$ be a classical solution. Define the global enstrophy $Q(t) := \|\omega(t)\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)}^2$. Then $Q(t)$ satisfies the exact differential identity:

$$\frac{1}{2} \frac{d}{dt} Q(t) + \nu \|\nabla \omega(t)\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} (S(x,t)\omega(x,t) \cdot \omega(x,t))\,dx, \tag{3.2}$$

where the boundary flux $\lim_{R \to \infty} \int_{\partial B_R} [ \frac{1}{2} |\omega|^2 (u \cdot \hat{n}) - \nu \omega \cdot (\nabla \omega \cdot \hat{n}) ] d\sigma = 0$ vanishes identically.

3.3. The Beale–Kato–Majda (BKM) Criterion

Theorem 3.1 (Beale–Kato–Majda, 1984):

A strong solution $u$ exists on $[0, T^*)$ and blows up at $T^* < \infty$ if and only if $\int_0^{T^*} \|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb{R}^3)}\,dt = \infty$. Consequently, if $\sup_{t \in [0, T^*)} Q(t) < \infty$, then $T^* = \infty$.
